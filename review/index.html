The acre library — the piece owns the ground Awaiting human

2026-08-14 · generator v9 · every gate green · visual judgement remains open

AcreLibrary.ts has claimed since it was written that "procedural code chooses arrangement only — it never invents a piece." It was not true: the solver synthesized every acre's edges from its neighbours' tiers, and the ground came from sixteen procedural shape functions in IslandRuntime. Two sessions of work under docs/ACRE_LIBRARY_PLAN.md made the comment true. The library is now 64 authored .acre text files, and what you see below is what those files draw.

This is one packet with three stages, because the second stage is only legible against the first. Same five seeds throughout, same camera, sampled from the field — height and water after the curve layer — so nothing here is a picture of the data. If it shows up in these frames, it is ground the player can walk to.

Stage 1 → 2 · the coastline stops being the acre grid

Eleven hand-written coastal pieces: coves, a spit, headlands, a keyhole bay, a lagoon, rounded and hooked corners. Plus the deletion of 26 files the bake had emitted as separate orientations of pieces the loader already rotates — four identical flat beaches, four identical corners — which had been inflating every variety measurement taken until then.

Stage 2 → 3 · the river and the interiors follow

Authoring only ever paid on the coast, because the coast was the only surface the piece owned. Rivers and ponds were still shaped by a bowed polyline through acre-border midpoints, so a delta or a meander drawn into a bitmap rendered as nothing. RIVER_FROM_PIECES is the same inversion COAST_FROM_PIECES already made — trace the bank from the cell mask, fit it with the same smoother, build a distance field on the same lattice. And CellTerrain gained dirt, so an interior acre can say something other than grass for the first time.

The vocabulary itself

Every authored piece, drawn from its own cells, with the weight that decides how often the placer reaches for it. This is the sheet to judge as a vocabulary: is this enough different kinds of acre to build an island out of, and is anything in here a mistake?

What the numbers did

  • Piece / drawn-ground agreement 97.62% → 98.45%. How much of the island the bitmap and the rendered surface agree about. river drawn dry fell from 2115 cells to 28; pond drawn dry from 80 to 12.
  • Shaped coastal acres 22.7% → 35.5%. Acres whose piece draws water somewhere a straight acre border could not — the direct measure of the old border-locked model retreating.
  • Piece variety, land only 66.7% → 79.6% minimum across 780 seed pairs; coastal 68.6% → 78.0%.
  • Solver cost: none. 1000-seed rejection histogram unchanged — 1748 attempts before and after, pieces/no-candidate 14.0% → 13.9%. Twenty-nine new pieces were free because every one kept its new shape inside its own borders.
  • Deleted: the socket span algebra, Acre.variant, channelDistance and its bow, two compatibility rules measured firing zero times in 455,680 border cells.

What to look for. (1) The coast: do the bays and points read as places — somewhere you would walk to — or as noise along an edge? (2) Proportion: shore-a, the plain beach, still carries weight 287 against 12–24 for each authored shape, so roughly two thirds of the coastline stays plain. Is that the right ratio, or should the authored shapes be commoner? It is one number per file. (3) The river: does it read as one watercourse across acre joins, or as segments? Its width now comes from the bitmap rather than from a constant, so it is wider than it was — is that width right? (4) The interiors: do the brown clearings read as places, and is their density right? (5) The delta at the river mouth — does it read, or is it fussy at this scale? This packet is not self-approved.

Known and deliberate: interior features cannot cross an acre border, so there are no paths — only clearings. The first drafts were paths, and every one stopped dead at its acre border and read as scattered dashes. A feature that must connect needs edge vocabulary and a routing pass, which is what the river has and what makes it work.

The soundtrack — 24 written tunes, a light kit, and four fixed cue bugs Awaiting human

2026-08-04 · judged by ear · play everything at /audio

The brief was a darker, moodier, less strictly structured score for Kindly Forward — Animal Crossing's abstract hourly themes and Tears of the Kingdom's looser writing, rather than POSTMARK's cheerful town-builder. The audit found the composer was not underdeveloped, it was optimised for the opposite goal: MUSIC_LEARNINGS.md records it being built to fix a five-second vamp by imposing maximal functional structure. "Cheerful" lives in its rules — triads only, a cadence every eight bars, I–V–vi–IV, mostly major modes, ~85% melodic density — so no change of motif could have escaped it. What follows is three tiers of work against that.

Tier 1 — what the notes are played on

  • Real instruments instead of bare oscillators. src/core/instruments.ts: additive struck tones with a separate decay rate per partial (felt piano, vibraphone, sub, reed), a Karplus–Strong pluck rendered once into a cached buffer, a three-oscillator detuned pad with a filter envelope, and a noise transient on every strike. The raw waveforms survive as named instruments, so the authored themes are note-for-note unchanged.
  • The room. A seeded procedural impulse response (3.2s, lowpassed at 2.6kHz on the return, with discrete early reflections) feeds one shared convolver. Every voice has a StereoPanner and its own send amount, and a limiter sits on the master. Before this the game had no effects whatsoever — everything was dry, centred, and summing straight into the destination.

Tier 2 — how the pieces are written rebuilt twice · 2026-08-04

Two rejected attempts precede this one, and both failures are worth keeping on the record because they were opposite mistakes.

  1. Ambient drift. Each hourly theme took a random slice of that hour's motif, gave it random note lengths, and rested for 4 + rand() * 8 beats over a tonic pedal. Rejected as "random notes over an ambient drone", which is a literal description of the algorithm. It also seeded off the theme name, so the four seasons of one hour were four unrelated pieces.
  2. Six authored bands. Six themes covered the day; the hour chose only texture and tempo. That fixed the seasons but made every hour indistinguishable — by design, since 13:00 and 15:00 were deliberately the same tune. Nobody can have a favourite hour among variations of one melody.

What is there now: twenty-four separately written tunes in src/core/hourlyThemes.ts, each with its own title, key, tempo, meter, feel, changes and hook. Twenty-four distinct melodies, eleven tempos from 103 to 167bpm, twelve keys, four waltzes, fourteen swung and ten straight, five lead instruments. The composer arranges and re-scores; it invents nothing and there is no RNG in the path.

Three things stopped it being ambient. The chords are comped, not padded — struck, short, and off the beat (Charleston figure for swing, anticipations for straight, two-and-three for the waltzes), so the space the melody leaves is actually empty instead of filled by a sustain. The bass walks — a note per beat under a swung tune, stepping through the chord and approaching the next root from a semitone away. The harmony moves: ii–V–I, secondary dominants, minor ii–V, tritone approaches, over maj7/min7/dom9/min7♭5 voicings.

The season invariant survived both rewrites and is asserted: all four seasons of an hour write one melody. A season may change key centre, tempo, comping instrument, chord density and reverb — but not the lead instrument, because that was tried and it flattens all twenty-four hours onto one timbre four times over, which is the six-band failure again.

The gate was rewritten with the intent, twice, and both deletions are named in the file so a reader can tell a reversal from a hole. One old assertion is now unsatisfiable by design: "no lead may contain both a major third and a major seventh" was a modal-mood rule, and a maj7 chord contains both.

Tier 2b — a kit, and a performance added 2026-08-04

The twenty-four tunes were accepted in direction with one gap named: no drums. The rhythm section was bass and comped chords — two of its three legs — and the swing feel was implied by where the bass walked rather than stated by anything. A ride cymbal states it in one bar.

The kit is light on purpose and absent on purpose. Every pattern is one bar and at most four hits; a straight-ahead swing ride pattern alone is six. That is the hardest constraint in the data, and it exists because the space between phrases is the whole difference between these tunes and the two rejected soundtracks — a drum kit is the fastest way in the world to fill it back in, and a busy pattern would undo the rewrite while sounding, bar to bar, like an improvement. Nine of the twenty-four hours have no kit at all, including an unbroken run from 01:00 to 04:00. Drums arriving at five and leaving again at two says something about the time of day that no pattern can, and a bare hour only reads as a decision if kits are otherwise normal.

  • Six drums, none of them notes. Kick, brush, ride, closed hat, rim, shaker. A kit voice has no key and does not transpose with the piece; its step indexes a palette instead of naming a pitch. Each is noise plus at most one pitched component — a membrane or a cymbal has a spectrum too dense and too inharmonic for additive synthesis, and the two things the ear uses to tell a hat from a ride are filter shape and decay time.
  • Velocity is not a second volume. Every note in the game was struck at one strength. The reason that reads as mechanical is not the level: an instrument struck softly is darker, because a gentler strike puts less energy into the upper partials, and scaling one gain gives the same spectrum quieter — which the ear hears as distance, not gentleness. Partials now fall off at different rates with velocity, and the attack transient fastest of all. It matters most under the drums: an unaccented pattern is a metronome.
  • The accents are metrical, and that is a limitation. They come from position in the bar, with the comp inverted so the syncopation is the accented hit — the point of a Charleston figure is that the push is not on the downbeat. Expressive dynamics (a crescendo across a phrase, a note leaned on because it is the top of the line) are still not expressible. That is the honest next gap.
  • A season may make the kit quieter and nothing else — not swap the pattern, not swap the drums, since the pattern is part of what makes an hour that hour. Indoors loses the kick: a room is a small space with a jukebox in it, and the low end is the first thing that stops making sense there.

Two review surfaces were lying, and this is the finding worth reading. The offline WAV renderer and the audio lab's piano roll each keep a copy of the scheduler loop. Both were faithful when written; both missed the swing correction (from "delay every odd cursor" to "delay whatever falls off the beat"), and both then played the new drum voices as pitched notes on a placeholder instrument. The output sounded entirely plausible. A bug in the game gets reported by a player; a bug in a preview is heard by the person deciding whether the work is good and answers the question wrongly with no signal at all. Both copies are corrected and now carry a comment naming this as the seam that has failed twice — but the real fix is to extract the loop so there is one, and that is not done.

Tier 3 — cues that were wrong

  • The unmute burst. Muted music left its clocks in the past, so re-enabling scheduled every missed note at once and WebAudio clamped them all to now — twenty seconds off came back as one chord. schedule() now walks cursor and clock forward silently first. Measured: 2 notes on unmute with the fix, 24 without.
  • Outdoor wildlife played indoors. Birds and crickets no longer spawn inside at all, and the ambience bus runs through a lowpass that closes to 520Hz. Muffling alone was not enough — a chirp filtered to 520Hz is still a chirp.
  • Heart scenes and the finale had no music. Both now set Game.sceneTheme, which currentTheme() consults first. It had to be an override rather than a setTheme() call, because updatePlay re-asserts the theme every frame and would have taken it straight back.
  • SFX bypassed the bus system. tone/noise/sweep/voiceBlip now land on an sfxBus with its own small send, and music ducks to 0.34 under dialogue.
  • New: a silence theme (so /story's fog can arrive with no musical hit and the previous cue leaves rather than stopping), and a per-role layer API so a scene can thicken or thin the arrangement without changing the tune.

Evidence

/audio is the packet. It plays all 192 hourly themes, the eight records, both staged cues, all twenty sound effects, the three footstep surfaces and the eight character voices — through the game's own AudioSystem, not a preview reimplementation, with the score drawn as the scheduler actually walks it. Layer and ambience controls are live.

Delivered separately as MP3. 1-a-tour-of-the-day (eleven hours, 30s each — the file that answers "every hour is indistinguishable") · 2-seasons-of-5pm (one tune, four scorings) · three full-length hours: 17 Going Home, 9 Errand Run, 3 Nightwatch Waltz.

The offline renderer no longer mirrors the synthesis in plain JS; it runs the real playNote / createReverbSend / connectVoice through a genuine OfflineAudioContext, with the impulse seeded so both sides of an A/B build the same room. Two gates were added — npm run test:music (192 themes, no browser) and npm run test:audio (boots the game) — and every assertion in both was confirmed red on a deliberate break before it was kept, per MUSIC_LEARNINGS.md §5. That cost two claims of mine: a line I had commented as load-bearing turned out to be dead code, and my first unmute threshold did not discriminate at all (5 vs 10) and now measures the note rate and scales to it.

Still open

  • The whole entry is the jury call. Nothing here is self-certified. Start with 1-a-tour-of-the-day: the test is whether you could argue for a favourite hour. The twenty-four tunes are hand-written and are the part most likely to be wrong; they are data, and any one of them can be rewritten without touching the arranger.
  • Everything below the notes is still unaddressed and is the larger remaining gap: every note is the same velocity, timing is a mathematically exact grid, and volume is gain only — so loud and soft notes are the same spectrum. That performance layer is now the biggest single lever on whether any of this stops sounding synthetic.
  • day, dusk and night are still unreachable dead code (~109 lines), and HOUR_MOTIFS (24 pitch-only clock melodies) joined them when the six band themes superseded it. Deleting authored material is a human call, so both are flagged in place rather than removed.
  • The authored themes never shared a grid, and still don't. The lab makes it plain: title's melody and bass are 74 and 44 beats, so they share a half-bar grid and only realign after 13m 34s; shop is a quarter-bar. This is §1's phasing pathology, never fixed for the hand-written themes because the composer replaced everything that mattered. title is the one still reachable, on the menu.
  • Interiors use one lead instrument at every hour of the day (pluck), where outdoors moves glass → felt → reed → glass. Surfaced by the lab's catalog column. Possibly intended — an interior should be less eventful — but it is the kind of thing that is a decision or an oversight and not obviously either.

Billboard sprite vs. 3D model — and a wrong number in the art direction Decision needed

2026-07-31 · investigation, not an asset gate · re-run after the texture pass · harness checked in

Built to settle whether Kindly Forward should use billboard sprites cut from the concept turnaround instead of low-poly models. The Postmaster's front figure was cut off the supplied sheet, billboarded, and stood beside the v04 model under one camera and one light rig. The model is lit by the scene; the sprite is emissive, because its lighting is already painted in.

The supplied sheet's scale figure is wrong

The turnaround sheet annotates ~24px as the in-game character height. This engine does not do that. src/world/CameraRig.ts:25 is a 35° vertical fov at distance 16, rendering into the 480×360 target from src/game/Game.ts:239. Frame height at that distance is 10.09 world units and the character is 2.59, so the real figure is about 90px — nearly four times the documented number. Every judgement made against "24px" so far has been made against the wrong target.

The finding, and the fix it produced

At true gameplay scale the model's face rendered blank — no eyes, just a cream oval. Measuring the atlas explained why, and it was not feature size: skin sits at 0.795 luminance while the eye band averages 0.619, with only 19% of its pixels genuinely dark. Downsample that 4:1 and each eye averages back into skin. The features were too light, not too small.

The texture pass that followed targets the gameplay read specifically: dark facial features are thickened and driven toward black so they survive averaging; coat trim was resized against the ~20px the coat front actually occupies on screen, which made the old 4px buttons and 10px belt about one screen pixel each; and the palette was warmed toward the turnaround's brown-black leather rather than pure ink. Same mesh, same 896 triangles — texture only.

Where this leaves the sprite question

  • The comparison is unfair in the sprite's favour and should not be read as settling it. That is a hero illustration — one frame, one angle, one pose. A sprite pipeline needs dozens of frames that agree with each other across directions and animation, which is the part an image-generation art source is worst at. A 3D rig gives that consistency by construction.
  • The largest gap it exposed was a texture problem, not a technology problem — and the before/after above is that claim tested rather than asserted. The gap narrowed substantially for texture work alone, with the rig, clips and export path untouched.
  • Compare what each side is still missing. The model's silhouette is already correct; what remains is surface polish. The sprite is missing an entire animation pipeline, and the frame-to-frame consistency needed to build one. Those are very different sizes of problem.
  • The sprite still reads warmer and carries real expression where the model has two dark marks. It has not been beaten — it has been closed on.

Evidence and harness: docs/review-queue/w5r-cast/postmaster/sprite-vs-model/ — build-comparison.py · extract-sprite.py · the cut sprite

Status: DECISION NEEDED. Two calls for a human: whether to correct the documented ~24px target wherever it has been relied on, and whether the post-pass gap is now small enough that 3D stays. Built by Claude in-session. The renders and the atlas luminance figures are measurements; which side of that comparison you prefer is not something this packet decides.

W5R — Postmaster form + texture candidate v04 Awaiting human

2026-07-31 · untextured clay abandoned · textured · 896 triangles · face baked into the head · texture pass targeted at gameplay scale

A different approach after three clay rounds failed to converge. v01 read as a helmet, v02 as a cocoon, v03 as split slabs — each satisfied its redline and lost the character elsewhere. The diagnosis: in DS-era chibi the head is a near-featureless mass and the identity lives in the paint, so a form-only gate was measuring the wrong thing. This candidate stops asking geometry to carry hair identity and is submitted textured, at the condition it actually ships in.

Hair is one open arc-shell whose gap is a crack at the crown and a channel at the face — it cannot enclose the face, because the opening is structural. The gap is solved rather than guessed: the hair's front lip sits at yo − cos(gap)·ry, and any value putting it ahead of the face plane hides the profile, which is what the clay rounds kept doing. The painted face atlas authored back in W4 is finally carried through. Placket, buttons, belt, buckles and wax seal are painted into the body sheet, not modelled.

This gate covers

  • Whether the textured read is the Postmaster from the supplied turnaround.
  • Hair as a single framing mass at 896 triangles, inside the 600–900 budget.
  • The head pass: crown radii sampled off an ellipsoid so the curve is continuous through the headband, the band dropped to the hairline so the whole crown sits above it, a wider face card, and a hair fall that tapers narrower than the coat so the body reads from behind.
  • Whether the true-side weakness is acceptable — it is inherent to a painted face card, and Animal Crossing has the same property, but that is a call to make rather than assume.

Known gaps

  • The body atlas is procedural — flat panels with placed marks, not painted fabric. It would be clearly better hand-painted the way the face was. The trim is now sized to be legible in play, but sizing is not the same as being well painted.
  • The baked face is a front projection, so it smears slightly across the cheeks in three-quarter. Clustering the head ring's samples toward the face cuts it but does not remove it — that is the cost of carrying a face on a 900-triangle skull with no card.
  • The headband still stands proud of the hair at the sides. It has to clear the hair's solidified surface plus its own facet sag, and at this side count that is roughly 0.03.
  • A faint seam runs down the crown where the part gap sits. Reads as a parting line here and disappears at gameplay scale.
  • No rig, skinning or clips. rig=not_started.

Where it landed

  • 16 × 12 acres, 224 × 168 units, a 14 × 10 interior of 140 placeable acres, land 67–78. Raised from 12 × 10, which was too tight to hold a plateau, a pond and a varied coast at once.
  • Structure per island: a plateau of 6–9 acres with one long cliff, a river of 9–15 acres, 3–6 bridge sites, 2 ramps, 2–10 cove stretches, 0–2 pond acres, 1–2 offshore islet acres.
  • Tier split runs t0 29–34 / t1 30–39 / t2 6–9 — the terrace is where the game is played, the shelf wraps it, the summit is a landmark you can cross in a few steps.
  • 60-seed sweep: every check passes, 60/60 reproducible. Cross-seed dissimilarity min 25.0%, mean 47.3% — reported, not gated, parked while the landmass structure was being reworked so it could not mask shape regressions.

The gate found none of this. A human did.

Twenty-four checks now run per island. Ten of them exist because someone looked at a picture and said that looks wrong, and in every case the mechanical gate had been green:

  • Water never climbs — the channel was descending to the shelf and climbing back onto the terrace.
  • A river leaves at the shelf — it was running five acres along a beach, which reads as a canal. Now 1 shelf acre on 57 of 60 seeds.
  • The river does not own the summit — it was lingering 8–12 acres on the plateau. Now 1–3, mean 2.4, out of 9–15.
  • No corner-only land — sand met sand at a diagonal, which is not walkable and sits in a different 4-connected region.
  • No wedge headlands — two diagonal corner pieces side by side made a point of land rather than a headland.
  • No one-acre bays — an ocean acre with three land neighbours is a notch the coast tears through.
  • A full land acre presents at most one ocean edge — square headlands read as staircases; two adjacent edges is now a diagonal half-sand piece.
  • No stranded land — a whole lobe could be unreachable and the old gate, which only checked the office, passed it.
  • Island compactness ≥52% — islands were coming out as two bulbs joined by a neck with the river forced through the join. Now 68–95%.
  • Plateau compactness ≥55% — the summit was a thin ribbon snaking across the terrace. Now 67–100%, mean 83%.

That is the argument for grey-box review before art, stated as plainly as the evidence allows: the mechanical gate has never once caught a shape problem first.

  • Coves were zero on every seed until the counts were printed. A beach acre almost always also carries a cliff socket inland where the shelf meets the terrace, and the fill pass tested cliff before the shore cases — so every coast acre was classified as a cliff. Now 2–10 per island.
  • Islets were missing on a quarter of seeds — a 0.75 probability gate plus a clearance test a large island leaves no room for. Now guaranteed and asserted.

W3 — Continuous generator rejected; island moves to an authored acre tileset Rejected · redirected

2026-07-29 · human decision · /island generation contract rewritten · direction: Animal Crossing spiritual successor, replayability first

The seeded blockouts below passed their gate 5/5 and were rejected on sight anyway. Reviewing the frames raised three objections — the rivers all looked alike, the summit had no elevation around it, and the dock frequently sat in open water — and probing the generator confirmed all three plus two more.

Measured defects across all five seeds

  • Dock in open ocean on 3 of 5 seeds. dock.z was hardcoded to 56 and never validated against the coastline, which varies from z≈47 to z≈63. Seed 2 put it 9.1 units out to sea. The post office had two fallback repairs; the dock had none.
  • River mouth past the coastline on 5 of 5. The spline ran a fixed z = -55 → +61, beyond the coast on every seed — against the contract's explicit "do not let the spline merely stop at the island edge".
  • Summit at beach height on 4 of 5. Measured heights 0.41, 0.57, −1.6, 0.55 against an intended 5.75. The −1.6 is the outside-the-mask sentinel: that summit was in the sea. The summit marker was placed independently of the terrain band it was supposed to crown.
  • Terraces identical on every seed. buildTerrain never called random(); all three bands were hardcoded constants, the high region a fixed circle at (29,−31) on every island.
  • No ramps anywhere. heightAt returned one of three constants, making band boundaries vertical walls of 1.9 and 3.4 units — against "ramps, not stairs" and "every essential destination has at least one ramped route".
  • Rivers near-identical by construction. Source always east (x ∈ [25,53]), z always monotonic north→south, shape a single sine hump with ±4.5 jitter on a 172-wide map. Not a tuning problem; a topology with one degree of freedom.

Why the gate passed all of it

island-check.mjs asserted bounds, bridge count, plot count, connectivity flags and reproducibility. It never asked whether an anchor was on land, and it never compared one seed to another — so it could not have caught either the floating dock or the sameness. That is the fifth gate on this project found measuring something other than the work.

Root cause, and the redirect

These were not six bugs. They were one: the mask, the dock, the river and the terraces were four independently-computed coordinate systems, and the code's job was to reconcile them afterward. Continuous geometry gives no guarantee that two separately-derived features agree.

The island now assembles from authored acre pieces on a fixed grid — 14-unit acres, a 12 × 10 grid, an always-ocean outer ring, three integer elevation tiers, and edge sockets that must match between neighbours. Every defect above becomes unrepresentable rather than fixed: a dock is a variant of a shore acre and cannot float; a river mouth is a shore acre; the summit sits on a tier-2 acre because height is a property of the acre; a tier change requires an authored ramp piece to exist. Replayability comes from arrangement — landmass growth, tier layout, dock choice, a free river path over the grid, bridge candidates, per-acre variants — and every reachable arrangement is valid by construction.

It also resolves a standing-rule violation. AGENTS.md rule 4 forbids procedural generation from being the asset; a sine-based coastline was exactly that.

What survives

  • Kept: IslandTraversal (bridge state, nav edges), PlotPlacement (player survey), IslandScatter (resource quotas), and the connectivity flood-fill approach.
  • Replaced: IslandLayout's mask and anchor selection, IslandTerrain entirely, IslandWater's spline.
  • New: AcreLibrary (pieces, sockets, variants), IslandSolver (nine passes), IslandGrid, IslandMesh.
  • Two gate assertions that were missing: anchors must be on legal acres, and different seeds must differ by ≥25% of acre assignments. Sameness is now a test failure.
