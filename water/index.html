Where the decisions live

src/world/water/WaterMaterials.ts
One registry, one tint passAll nine surfaces register with a tint family (fresh, ocean, foam). update(daylight, wetness, dt) composes day/night, season and rain in a fixed order for every member at once. A surface that forgets to register is invisible to the pass, and a test walks the roster to catch that.
src/world/water/ChannelFlow.ts
Which way a river runsBreadth-first from the river mouths over the acre graph; channelUv projects world position onto the downstream direction so v runs with the current. Ponds and basins pass null and keep planar UVs, because still water has no direction to claim.
src/world/water/ShoreLap.ts
The foam along the waterlineOne ribbon, one draw, marched along smoothedCoast with cell-scale Chaikin smoothing, mitred point normals, a crowding index that skips doubled-back coast, and a drape onto the ground so the beach cannot clip it. 1,878 triangles for the whole island.
src/world/water/Buoyancy.ts
Things that floatA module-level registry; a sine on y plus a slower roll and a half-rate pitch. One client today: the ferry.
src/world/modules/WorldFoundation.ts
Where the water is builtbuildIslandWater emits the ocean as two-unit quads over ocean cells plus four coarse aprons to the horizon, and the fresh sheet as half-unit quads wherever ground is genuinely under its own surface. Both carry depth as a vertex colour.
src/world/island/IslandRuntime.ts
The shape under the watercoastProfile is one continuous function of signed distance to the traced coast: apron inland over APRON_RUN 2.5, shelf seaward over SHELF_RUN 5, meeting at sea level by construction. WADE_DEPTH 0.35 decides where a walker may still stand.
src/world/terrain.ts
The ground's opinion about waterSubmerged ground paints in the deep-water colour shaded by depth; a 0.35-unit height window above the surface paints the damp band, graded, as darker sand rather than as a third colour.
src/world/modules/WorldRuntime.ts
The per-frame motionFour texture offsets and one opacity: river scroll, ocean scroll and cross-drift, falls scroll, lap slide along the shore, and the lap's two-sine breath. Plus updateBuoyancy.
src/core/RetroMaterial.ts
The swellSWELL_CHUNK — two long sines in world space, 39 and 57 units, opposed drift, amplitude WATER_SWELL = 0.045 u. Keyed into customProgramCacheKey, which is the trap: a variant that patches the shader without extending that key silently reuses another program.

Six findings

Each of these is a measurement first and a judgement second. None is built. They are ordered by how much of the frame they change per line of code.

Finding 1 · bug · WorldFoundation.buildIslandWater

The ocean's texture is tiled forty times too fine to be seen

The ocean sheet carries world-space UVs: seaQuad pushes px / ACRE_SIZE, and the mesh's u was measured spanning −28…28 across x −392…392, which is exactly x/14. On top of that, oceanTex.repeat is set to (40, 80) — measured live — because it was sized for the 560-unit PlaneGeometry that used to be the sea and carried normalised 0…1 UVs.

The plane was replaced during the shore work. The repeat was not. Multiplied together, one tile of a 128 × 256 stroke sheet now covers 0.35 world units across and 0.175 along — a third of a footstep.

With LinearMipmapLinearFilter and anisotropy 1, that samples a mip where the sheet is a handful of texels: the sea resolves to the average colour of its own texture. There is no stroke pattern on the open water in any frame this survey captured, and the vertical colour profile down ocean-day.png confirms it — #3a3925 at the top of the frame to #40402b at the waterline, a 2% drift across the entire sea.

And the motion goes with it. oceanTex.offset.y = (now / 45000) % 1 advances one tile per 45 seconds. One tile is 0.175 u, so the sea drifts at 3.9 millimetres per second — 0.09 internal pixels per second. The cross-drift, sin(now / 11000) * 0.012, has an amplitude of two millimetres. Even if the strokes were visible, they would not appear to move. The swell in the vertex shader is real and does move; it is the only motion the open sea has.
Finding 2 · bug · scripts/grade-palette.mjs, src/art/swatches.generated.ts

The palette grade takes the blue out of the water, and out of the light on it

grade-palette.mjs applies the Era 1 art direction to every swatch in the generated table, once, destructively. Each channel is pulled 22% toward the RGB average, then gained and lifted per channel — and blue gets the smallest of both, deliberately: the comment reads "pull saturation toward warm paper, then age blue slightly more than red."

muted   = average + (channel − average) × 0.78
red     = muted[0] × 0.96 + 8
green   = muted[1] × 0.93 + 7
blue    = muted[2] × 0.88 + 5

That is a defensible grade for brass, timber, grass and sand — materials whose read is texture and value. Water has no texture and no value of its own. Hue and motion are all it has, and the grade removes most of one of them:

The last three rows are the ones that matter most and are easiest to miss. The ocean is a horizontal Lambert surface, so the largest single term in its shading is the hemisphere light's sky colour — and that has been graded from a cool blue-white to a warm off-white. The sea is now a nearly-neutral tint, multiplied by a nearly-neutral map, lit by warm light, multiplied again by a 0.70 depth shade.

The measured result, and the giveaway. Open sea at noon, summer, clear, sampled over a 300 × 30 px block: #3d3c27 — red 61, green 60, blue 39. The same block at 23:00: #0e2e4a, a real navy. The night sea is blue and the day sea is not, because at night the sun and the sky bounce fall away and the tint's own residual blue survives. And palette.ts no longer describes the build: it says ocean.day = h8fb0c0, the running material holds #97aaaa, and anyone tuning the sea by editing that literal is editing a number that gets rewritten downstream.
Finding 3 · gap · IslandRuntime.shelfHeight, the shipped .island

The sea has one depth, and the shallows are 2.7% of it

OCEAN_FLOOR is a constant −1.4 and shelfHeight eases from sea level down to it over SHELF_RUN = 5 units of distance from the traced coast. So the sea has exactly two regions: a five-unit ring, and everything else at one number.

The surface repeats the claim. depthShade writes a per-vertex multiplier saturating at DEPTH_FULL 1.6, and 94.4% of the ocean sheet's 76,410 vertices land inside a single 0.034-wide band of it — 0.694 to 0.728, against a full range of 0.66 to 1.0 (measured spread 0.702–1.000, mean 0.711). The river, whose bed follows riverCarveAt, puts 66.6% in one such band and spreads the rest across three more. The tool to fix this shipped: W11 added a # depth: block to .acre, a depth layer in the /acre editor, and a smoothed per-island depth field. The shipped island does not use it.

Why it costs more than it sounds. A player standing on the beach reads the sea in bands. New Leaf gives them at least three from a standing camera — wet sand, a light walkable shallow, the deep — and the boundaries between them are where the eye rests. We have two bands, the shallow one is one shade wide and 2.7% of the water by area, and the rest is a single flat field taking up more than half the frame.
Finding 4 · bug · TownMap.makeWaterTexture

Both water sheets are drawn from unseeded noise, so no two runs photograph the same sea

makeWaterTexture calls Math.random() for stroke position, length, curvature, width and colour — 40 strokes for the ocean, 60 for fresh water — on every page load. Measured, two cold loads of the same build:

waterTex hash4fcd1623 → 18501a81
oceanTex hash3741a70e → e9cba2ce
lapTex hash (seeded)c5f3bc20 → c5f3bc20

The shore lap seeds its own generator and hashes identically. Its comment states the rule outright — "a texture that resamples every load makes a visual regression impossible to compare against its own baseline" — and cites makeFallTexture as the precedent. The two oldest water textures in the codebase are the two that break it.

This one gates the others. Every proposal below is judged by eye, which means a before/after pair. As long as the sea repaints itself on every load, a pair of frames of the water surface is not a comparison and the jury is being asked to compare two different textures. Fixing this is a four-line change and it should land before anything else that touches the sheets.
Finding 5 · bug · TownMap.makeWaterTexture, ChannelFlow.channelUv

The river's strokes still lie across the current — eight to one

ChannelFlow exists because the fresh sheet used to take planar world UVs and scroll due north on every island. Its header names two symptoms: the current ran the wrong way, "with the texture's own strokes lying across the current as well." The first was fixed. The second was not.

channelUv returns u across the channel and v downstream, both divided by ACRE_SIZE. waterTex.repeat is (1, 4). So one tile is 14 units across and 3.5 along, carrying a 128 × 256 sheet: 9 texels per world unit across the current, 73 along it. A round stroke on the canvas becomes an ellipse eight times wider than it is long — stretched across the flow, crushed along it.

And the comment above it disagrees with the line below it. makeWaterTexture reads: "Fresh water only. One tile per 14x14 acre quad, which is the size every river and pond surface is built at." The next line sets repeat.set(1, 4). The channel is 4.4 units wide, so the sheet gives it about a third of a tile across — the pattern the player actually sees on the river is one third of one stroke, spread wide.
Finding 6 · absence · FishingSystem, DivingSystem

Nothing lives in the water

MAX_SHADOWS is 4, and maintainShadows places them by rejection-sampling anywhere in the map bounds that is wet. Four shadows over 21,710 wet cells. DivingSystem keeps four more, but only while the player is swimming, and clears them on exit.

So on an ordinary walk along the coast, the expected number of visible fish shadows is approximately zero — and the fish are the main reason to look at water in this genre at all. Nothing else is on the surface either: no birds, no drifting debris, no jumping fish, no boat traffic. The ferry floats, and it is the only object the sea carries.

Cheapest large win on this page. The shadows already know how to stay in water, steer back when they land dry, and sit at the right height for ocean versus fresh. What they do not do is exist near the player. A budget raised to a few dozen, spawned in a ring around the camera focus instead of over the map, on one instanced mesh, changes how alive the water feels more than any shader on this list.

New Leaf — eight things it does, and where we stand

This column is what a player sees, not a claim about how Nintendo built it. It is here because "make it more like Animal Crossing" is only actionable once it is broken into things that can be measured against our own build.

1 · The sea is blue against warm sand

Hue contrast between water and shore does most of the work of saying "coast" at a glance, before any wave or foam is drawn. The sand is warm, the water is cool, and the boundary between them is the picture.

Us: measured #3d3c27 sea against #b08543 sand, in the same frame. HSL saturation 0.22 for the water, 0.45 for the sand; hue 57° and 36° — both warm, one family, forty degrees apart. The one contrast that reads for free, we have collapsed. Finding 2.

2 · The shallows are their own colour, wide enough to stand in

There is a legible light band between the wet sand and the deep, and the player can walk into it. It is the reason a New Leaf beach reads as somewhere you can go rather than as a boundary.

Us: the wadeable band exists — WADE_DEPTH 0.35, about 1.25 units of shelf — but it carries one shade and covers 2.7% of the sea. Finding 3.

3 · The surf advances and retreats

The waterline itself moves: foam runs up the sand and drains back on a cycle you can time, and the wet strip it leaves behind is what makes the beach look damp.

Us: the ribbon is fixed in place and breathes opacity between 0.30 and 0.70, twice a cycle, from two detuned sines. Sliding it inland was tried and tears, because the texture's v is clamped for good reason. A wave that fades in place reads as a light on the sand rather than as water. The damp band beneath it is correct and graded and does not move either.

4 · Water is animated at a scale you can see

Ripples are about a hand's width and travel at roughly walking pace. Both facts matter: too fine and it is noise, too slow and it is a still image.

Us: 0.35-unit tiles drifting at 3.9 mm/s on the sea, and an 8:1 anisotropic pattern on the river. Findings 1 and 5. The vertex swell is the only motion that survives, and it is 4.5 cm.

5 · The water is full of life

Fish shadows are everywhere, all the time, in every body of water, at every hour — the surface is never empty, and that is what makes it worth walking along.

Us: four shadows, placed anywhere on the map. Finding 6.

6 · Waterfalls are bright banded curtains with a mist base

A falls is one of the few places the palette goes near white, and it reads as vertical energy: banding down the sheet, a bright lip where the river tips over, spray that hides the join at the bottom.

Us: a flat pale rectangle with a scrolling map, a lip pair built to be invisible, and 28 spray droplets in one buffer. Structurally sound; visually unaddressed. Outstanding.

7 · The hue never leaves blue

Hour and weather move the sea's value and temperature a long way — dusk, storm, night — but never so far that it stops being blue. The blue is the constant the rest is varied against.

Us: the day/night lerp works, the winter tint works, rain darkens correctly across all nine surfaces. What leaves is the blue itself, at noon, in clear summer weather — the most common frame in the game.

8 · The camera is fixed, and ours is not

A fixed near-orthographic camera keeps the waterline in a narrow, predictable depth band and never shows it edge-on.

Us: we orbit, and we snap vertices to a 480 × 360 grid. That is not a defect — it is a licence difference. We are more exposed to coplanar surfaces at the waterline than New Leaf is, which is why the last audit's z-fighting was so severe and why the current "sea built where the sea is" approach has to stay.

The slate

Ten proposals in three lanes. Lane 1 is a day's work and unblocks the rest; lane 2 decides whether the sea reads as water; lane 3 is what makes it feel inhabited. Every item marked [EYES] ends in REVIEW_QUEUE.md for a human, per the jury rule.

P1 · lane 1 · WorldFoundation.buildIslandWater

Delete the ocean's texture repeat, then re-derive the scroll in world units

The sheet's UVs are already world-space at one tile per acre. Removing oceanTex.repeat.set(...) restores the scale the stroke sheet was drawn for and makes the sea's texture visible for the first time since the plane was replaced. Then the scroll has to be re-expressed: today it is "one tile per 45 seconds", which is meaningless once the tile changes size. State it as world units per second and pick a number a player can see — the swell is the reference, not the clock.

Expect a follow-up. The stroke sheet has never been looked at near its intended scale, and it may well be wrong there. That is the point: this change makes the texture a thing that can be art-directed instead of a thing that averages to grey.

Gate: measured world period of one tile, and measured world speed of the scroll. [EYES] — a beach frame at noon, against the same frame today.

P4 · lane 2 · WaterMaterials, swatches.generated.ts, DayNight

Give water its blue back — and decide whether water is lit at all

Two halves, and they should be decided together.

The tints. Three options: exempt the water family from ERA1_GRADED; re-author the literals in palette.ts as post-grade values with a comment saying so; or give WaterMaterials its own colour constants outside the generated table. Recommend the third. The registry exists precisely to be the one opinion about what water colour is, and a module that owns the decision should own the numbers. It also ends the situation where palette.ts states a colour the build does not use.

The light. The hemisphere sky term is the largest contributor to a horizontal Lambert surface, and it has been graded warm. Either pre-compensate the tint for it, or make the two water sheets MeshBasicMaterial — which is what the two falls curtains, the plunge foam and the lap ribbon already are, and closer to how New Leaf’s water reads. Unlit water also removes a whole class of surprises where the sea changes hue because the sun moved. The cost is that the vertex swell stops catching light, which at 4.5 cm of amplitude it barely does.

Gate: the measured sea pixel at noon and at 23:00, reported as hue and saturation, with an explicit target: the day sea must not be less saturated than the sand it meets. [EYES] — all six survey framings, before and after.
medium — touches a global gradethe largest visual change on this pagea design call, not just a fix
P5 · lane 2 · IslandRuntime, the shipped .island

Give the sea a shallow zone with real width

Two moves. Widen the default SHELF_RUN so the ring is legible from a standing camera — and note the ceiling: coastGrid clamps distance at BEACH_WIDTH 6, so past that the field saturates and a longer run flattens into a hard edge. Getting more than that means the clamp moves too.

Then author it. W11 shipped a # depth: block and a depth layer in /acre, and the island does not use either. A painted lagoon, a drop-off off the headland and a shallow bar across the river mouth would do more for the sea than any of the shader work here.

Gate: shelf width per coast crossing, median and p90; fraction of the sea shallower than 0.40 u, currently 2.7%. [EYES].
code small, content realthe tool already exists
P6 · lane 3 · ShoreLap, RetroMaterial

Make the waterline move instead of fade

The ribbon already carries v across the band, seaward to inland. Displace it along its own band axis per-vertex in the shader, phase-offset by u so a wave travels along the shore instead of the whole island pulsing. The tear that blocked this was a texture-space problem — repeating v wraps the bright core back in at the inland edge — and moving geometry does not wrap.

Two warnings. The band is draped onto the ground, so the displacement has to preserve the drape or the foam will climb through the beach. And the swell already keys customProgramCacheKey; a new variant must extend that key or the patch will silently never compile.

Gate: measured travel of the foam's inland edge, in world units, over one cycle. [EYES].
shader work on an existing meshno new draw calls
P7 · lane 3 · FishingSystem

Fish, everywhere, on one instanced mesh

Raise the shadow budget from 4 to a few dozen and spawn them in a ring around the camera focus rather than over the map bounds, the way the rain field and the moon path already follow the view. One InstancedMesh, culled by inWater, recycled as the player walks. The steering, the height rule and the flee behaviour are all already written and all already correct.

Keep the caught-fish logic reading from the same list, so a bigger population does not become a second opinion about what is catchable.

Gate: shadows visible within 20 units of the player at ten sampled coastal positions, currently ~0. One draw call for the whole population. [EYES].
smalllargest felt change per line
P8 · lane 3 · WorldFoundation.buildCascades

Make the falls a curtain

Vertical banding down the sheet at a world scale that survives the framebuffer, a bright lip where the river tips over, and a mist card at the base to hide the join. The structure is already right — one draw, unlit, correct rain response, spray along the whole impact line rather than from a point — so this is texture and one or two quads, not a rebuild.

Gate: none mechanical. [EYES], and it is the frame in this survey that most needs a designer before a programmer.
art-ledneeds a design pass first
P9 · lane 3 · WorldFoundation, .island format

Point the swell at the beach

Derive a per-island swell heading from the mean outward normal of the traced coast, or author one as a # swell: heading beside the depth block. Then the sea runs onto the shore instead of along it. Note the limit honestly: one heading for a whole island is right for a small island and wrong for a bay, and the correct version is a field, not a constant — but a constant is a large improvement over world Z.

Gate: angle between the swell direction and the local coast normal, sampled around the island. [EYES].
mediumdesign call embedded
P10 · lane 3 · buildIslandWater

Make the ocean's 25,470 triangles pay, or spend them elsewhere

The near-field sea is tessellated at two units, which costs 25,470 triangles — 17.6% of the whole world — and currently buys a 4.5 cm swell and a vertex colour with 94% of its samples in one bucket. That is a fair price if the tessellation carries something: shoreline-parallel wave shading, a real depth gradient once P5 lands, foam accumulating in the troughs. If it is not going to carry any of that, coarsen the near field and put the budget into P6 and P7.

Decide this after P1, P4 and P5, not before. Two of those change what the surface has to say, and the answer to "how finely should we tessellate the sea" depends entirely on that.

  5. P10 — revisit the ocean's triangle budget last.
One sequencing argument worth stating. P4 is the largest change and the temptation is to do it first. It should not be: as long as the ocean's texture averages to grey (P1) and the sheets repaint on every load (P2), a palette change is being judged against a surface that has no pattern and no stable baseline. Fix what the eye can measure, then change what only the eye can judge.

This is a survey and a slate, not a report of work. No code changed for it. The only files it produced are the six frames and the JSON in docs/review-queue/water/survey-0827/.

Including the frames above. Five water items — 00W through 00W6 — are still open in REVIEW_QUEUE.md awaiting a human, and this page adds nothing to that queue and settles nothing already in it.

The New Leaf column is observational.

It describes what a player sees in that game. It makes no claim about how it was implemented, and nothing here should be read as a description of Nintendo's engine.

One pixel is one measurement.

#3d3c27 is a 300 × 30 block of one frame, at one camera, on one island, in summer, clear weather, at noon. The mechanism behind it — graded tint × graded map × 0.70 depth shade × warm sky light — is read from the code and each factor was measured separately. The exact contribution of each to that pixel was not isolated, and a per-term ablation is the first thing P4 should do.

Performance numbers are headless.

698 draw calls and 312,408 triangles per frame were measured in headless Chrome at 640 × 480 over 60 frames at the beach spot, with renderer.info.autoReset disabled. Triangle counts include every pass, not just the scene.

Findings 1 and 5 are arithmetic, not opinion.

The UV spans, the repeat values, the texture dimensions and the filter modes were all read from the live meshes. The conclusion that the sea resolves to its own average colour follows from those numbers and from mipmapping with anisotropy 1; it is also visible in the frames, but it does not depend on them.
