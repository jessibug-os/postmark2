Anatomy — what grass is made of today

One baked sheet, one tiled detail sheet, one whole-material season multiply. Three numbers decide everything you see: 8 texels per world unit on the base, 80 on the detail, 35.7 screen pixels per world unit in the buffer they land in.

1792 × 1344baked ground sheet, 8 texels per world unit
256²detail sheet, tiled every 3.2 u — 80 texels per unit
35.7 pxone world unit across, in the 480 × 360 buffer
0ground-cover meshes; no tufts, no clover, nothing standing in it
The scale split nobody wrote down. The base sheet magnifies 4.5× on its way to the screen, so nothing it paints below about 2 world units survives as anything but a blur. The detail sheet minifies 2.2×, so anything it paints above ~10 cycles per tile lands at or under the buffer's Nyquist limit and mips away to grey. The band a player can actually see is roughly 0.3 u to 2 u — and neither sheet is currently aimed at it.

Where the decisions live

src/world/terrain.ts · paintIslandGround
The base colour, per texelWalks 2.4M texels. Every branch is a consequence of the surface — depth, slope, wet band, bank feather — except the last one, which is the lawn: put(i, tier[i] >= 2 ? h6f7a44 : h3c4a30, 1 + tier[i] * 0.12). Two hexes and a multiplier, and tier is where the trouble is.
src/world/island/IslandField.ts · tierAt
The elevation the painter readslocate(x, z) floors the point into the acre grid and returns acre.tier. It is a per-acre integer with no interpolation, which is correct for what it is for — deciding which piece you are standing on — and wrong for shading a continuous surface.
src/world/terrain.ts · makeGroundDetailTexture
The blade sheet, which does existThree channels: R clumps + cross-hatch for grass, G wind ripple for sand, B neutral breakup for everything. It is well made. Its grass channel is authored to swing ±0.29, and on grass it delivers ±0.014.
src/world/terrain.ts · applyGroundDetail
The classifier that turns it offgrassMask = smoothstep(0.0, 0.09, diffuseColor.g - diffuseColor.r), in linear space. It asks the pixel what material it is instead of being told, and the answer for the shipped grass swatch is 0.012 — so the mask is 0.05.
src/game/Game.ts · SEASON_GRADE
The whole year, as one multiplymap.setSeasonTint(g.ground) sets groundMat.color. A multiply can only take light away, so summer is the brightest grass the game can render and every other season is a dimmer version of it. palette.seasons.*.ground — the swatches that look like they are for this — are read by nothing.
src/world/weather/WetnessField.ts
The machine a wear field would reuseA per-cell accumulator, uploaded as a data texture, read at cell centres and quantised to fixed levels in the ground shader. Exactly the plumbing path wear needs, already built, already debugged, already in groundUniforms.

Five findings

All measured against the live build on the shipped map. F1 and F2 are defects with a specific line of code behind them; F3 is a consequence of a decision that was probably right in the small; F4 and F5 are absences.

bug · F1 · this is the one in the screenshot

The lawn is shaded from an acre lookup, so the acre lattice is visible on flat ground

Grass brightness is 1 + tier × 0.12 and tier comes from tierAt, which floors the world point into the acre grid and returns that acre's integer. So the 12% step falls on the acre border — a straight line 14 units long — regardless of where the ground actually changes height. On the shipped island the two never coincide.

Grass border where the paint steps and the ground does not (< 0.4 u)56.5 u
Grass border where the paint steps and the ground does too0.0 u
Distinct borders affected6
Worst single border — river-straight t1 against cliff-transition t013.5 u
Why it happens. A cliff-transition or cliff-ramp acre carries one tier but contains two heights — the climb happens inside it. The flat ground at the top of that climb belongs to a tier-0 acre and is painted tier-0, while the identical flat ground one texel across the border belongs to a tier-1 acre and is painted 12% brighter. Nothing is wrong with the geometry. The paint is answering a different question than the one it looks like it is answering.

The fix is one line, and it is already in scope. height[i] is filled in the same loop, three lines above tier[i], and it is the continuous field. The tier read can go.
bug · F2

The blade-detail sheet is switched off on grass by a colour test the Era 1 palette grade invalidated

applyGroundDetail works out what material a pixel is by looking at its colour: smoothstep(0, 0.09, g − r) in linear space. That threshold was chosen for a saturated green. The Era 1 grade then pulled every generated swatch 22% toward its own RGB average — the grade palette.ts describes as "the right grade for brass, timber, grass and sand" — and grass came out at g − r = 0.012.

Read the second row. Tier-2 grass scores 0.053 grass and 0.517 sand. The high lawn is being given wind ripples ten times more strongly than blades.

And there is a frame that proves it. In lawn-noon.png below, the sand band and the grass are the same sheet at the same tile size in the same shot. The sand shows ripples you can count. The grass shows nothing.

What the lawn is left with. With the blade channel at 0.05, the sheet may move a grass texel by ±0.0145 from the blades and ±0.08 from the B channel — the neutral breakup that every surface gets, rock and riverbed included. 85% of the only variation on the lawn is the channel authored specifically not to be about grass.

Why patching the threshold is the wrong fix. The classifier is re-deriving a fact the painter already had in hand and threw away. paintIslandGround knows exactly which branch it took for every texel, and the sheet's alpha channel is written 255 everywhere and read by nothing.
gap · F3

Every grass colour the game can produce, in any season, is olive

Twelve reachable grass colours — three tiers × four season multiplies. Their hue spans 52° to 78°. Green is 120°. Their saturation spans 0.19 to 0.34 and their luma spans 0.196 to 0.301: the whole year of grass fits inside a tenth of the value range.

Spring, tier 0#3c4027 · h70 s.24 l.24
Summer, tier 0 (brightest possible)#41462d · h72 s.22 l.26
Autumn, tier 0#37341b · h54 s.34 l.20
Winter, tier 0#3a3f2a · h74 s.20 l.23
This is not an argument for Nintendo green. CLAUDE.md asks for a muted earthy palette and it is right to. But muted and monochrome are different decisions, and only one of them was made deliberately. The centre of mass is fine; the spread is zero.

The season multiply cannot help. A material colour multiply can only take light away, so summer's #fdf4e5 is the ceiling and autumn is the same lawn dimmed 22%. It is the identical argument applyGroundDetail already makes for snow — "multiplying green grass by pale blue just gives dim green" — and snow was given a shader blend for exactly that reason. Grass was not.
dead · F4

palette.seasons.*.ground is read by nothing

Four authored ground swatches — spring #8a9a5a, summer #7d8f5c, autumn #8a6b45, winter #a8bcc8. They are the only place in the palette where somebody wrote down what grass should look like across a year. grep finds two readers of palette.seasons in the whole tree, both of them debug overlays, and both reading .accent. The ground season colour actually in use is Game.SEASON_GRADE[...].ground, a separate table of white-ish multipliers in a 3,576-line file.

Two tables answering one question, and the one that reads like the answer is the dead one. Whatever the model ends up being, this should end with a single owner.
gap · F5

Grass has one length scale, and it is the wrong one

The only variation on the lawn is the detail sheet, tiled every 3.2 u. There is no metre-scale patchiness, no wear, and nothing standing in the grass — no tufts, no clover, no weeds. Every InstancedMesh in the build belongs to shadows, particles or fish.

And the sheet carries a grid. Every noise function in terrain.ts — tileNoise, edgeNoise, the wetness field's smoothNoise — is value noise sampled on the world axes. Value noise puts its extrema on lattice lines, so several octaves stacked in one frame read as a plaid rather than as a field. At the shipped 5% mask nobody can see it; run the same sheet at full strength, as the studio panel does, and it appears immediately. It is AGENTS.md rule 4 one level down — procedural standing in for design, and reading mechanical because of it. A low-frequency domain warp and a per-octave rotation take it out, and both are a handful of multiplies at bake time.

The scale problem. Even with F2 fixed, the sheet's own content is aimed too high. Of the grass channel's authored swing, the clump term (8 cycles/tile = 14 screen px per cycle) survives; the fine term (26 cycles = 4.4 px) and the grit (40 cycles = 2.9 px) sit at or under the buffer's Nyquist limit and average to grey — or crawl when the camera moves. Roughly half the authored amplitude cannot reach the screen at any mask value.

Proposed

The ramp this proposes

Four stops instead of two hexes. The centre of mass is deliberately left where the Era 1 grade put it — mean hue 78°, mean luma 0.27, the same lawn — and the spread is put back. Position along the ramp comes from the macro field, so hue and value move together the way a real field's does rather than as one brightness knob.

Where each layer has to live

Set by the scale split in the anatomy, not by preference. Above 2 units the base sheet is the only thing with the reach; below 1 unit it is the only thing without the resolution.

Feature size in world units, against what can carry it. The shaded band is what a player at the default camera can actually resolve.

Reference — what the games we are copying actually do

Stated as observation of the shipped games, not as documentation of them. The point of the list is that none of these is expensive, and we have the machinery for four of the six already.

A repeating ground motif

The ground carries a visible small-scale pattern that repeats on the tile grid and is never hidden. It is not photoreal grass and does not try to be — it is a legible texture with a recognisable beat, which is what makes the ground feel like a surface rather than a fill colour.

We have it. The detail sheet is exactly this and it is switched off.

Things standing in it

Weeds, clover, flowers and small plants sit on the ground as separate objects at a density that reads as ground cover rather than as props. They catch the light differently from the ground, which is what gives the lawn depth at player height.

We have none. Biggest single win available, one draw call.

Wear where people walk

Repeated traffic changes the ground under it, and stopping changes it back. It is the clearest signal a town has been lived in, and it costs no art — it is a field that accumulates.

We have the machine. WetnessField is a per-cell accumulator with a shader path already wired into groundUniforms.

Seasonal colour, not seasonal dimming

The ground's hue changes with the season — spring's yellow-green, high summer's deeper green, autumn's warmth. It is a different colour, not the same colour with less light on it.

We dim. F3. The swatches for the real version are already authored and dead (F4).

Blended material edges

Grass meets sand and dirt through a scatter of the one into the other, not on a line.

We half have it. coverageField and EDGE_WOBBLE already push the contour off the lattice; what is missing is stipple across the boundary.

Large-scale patchiness

A field is not one colour at ten metres. Broad soft variation across a whole area is what stops a lawn reading as a painted plane in wide shots — which is most shots, at this camera.

We have none, and the base sheet is the one thing with the reach to carry it. Free: the painter already visits every texel.

The honest comparison. Of six things, two are defects in work already done (F1, F2), two are cheap additions to machinery we already own (macro variation, wear), one is a table swap (season), and one is genuinely new (ground cover). None of it is a rewrite, and none of it needs the asset pipeline NINTENDO_GAP.md is waiting on.

Two calls this page is making

Call 1 · replaces the tier read entirely

Elevation should stop tinting the lawn at all

The 1 + tier × 0.12 multiplier is trying to say high ground catches more light. That is a real idea and it belongs to the lighting model, not to the ground's albedo — the sun already knows where the high ground is, and a baked brightness ramp fights it at every hour that isn't noon. Layer 0 fixes the seam by making the read continuous; this call goes one step further and asks whether the read should exist.

What it frees. Grass becomes one ramp driven by one field (the macro mottle), instead of a ramp times an elevation term that nothing else in the world agrees with. If the uplands should read differently, that is a different ramp position — drier, paler, more worn — which the macro field can carry for free, and which is a statement about what the high ground is rather than about how much light hits it.

subtractiveunblocks layer 1
Call 2 · the general shape of F2

A shader must never infer a material the baker already knew

F2 is not a bad threshold. It is a classifier standing where a fact should be, and it stayed silently wrong through a palette change that had no reason to know it existed. The ground sheet's alpha byte is written 255 for 2.4M texels and read by nobody.

The rule worth writing down: if the bake knows it, the bake writes it. That makes the palette grade safe to re-run, which is the actual failure this page found — not a wrong number, but a coupling nobody could see.

no new memorymakes the grade re-runnable
Call 3 · scope

Ground cover is the biggest win and it is still third

Tufts standing in the grass would do more for this than everything else combined. It is deliberately behind the four free layers, because a lawn with tufts on it and a visible acre seam underneath is a lawn with a visible acre seam, and because the tuft density wants the macro field (layer 1) to have something to say.

Budget, measured — and it only works one way. A static island-wide scatter at a density that reads (one tuft per 0.45 u, which is what the studio panel draws) needs about 33,000 instances to cover 6,760 u² of land, and most of them are behind the camera. A fixed pool re-anchored to the camera holds the count constant instead: 6,000 instances over a 30 × 24 u footprint is the same density where it is seen, ~24,000 triangles against the world's current 161,834, and one draw call against 679 meshes. The instancing pattern is already in Shadows.ts.

The slate

Three lanes. Lane one is defect repair and can be verified by a script. Lanes two and three are visual and cannot — they end in the review queue under the jury rule, like everything else that is judged by eye.

1 · Stop the bleedingDefects. Gate-verifiable. No new assets.
  1. Shade grass from height[i], not tierAt. Kills all 56.5 u of false seam. One line in paintIslandGround.
  2. Write the material id into the ground sheet's alpha and delete grassMask/sandMask from applyGroundDetail. Restores the blade sheet from 0.05 to 1.0.
  3. Add scripts/grass-gate.mjs with the three assertions below, so neither defect can come back through a palette change.
  4. Rebalance makeGroundDetailTexture's R channel out of the terms that land under Nyquist, and warp its sample point so the value-noise lattice stops showing.
2 · Give it grainVisual. Bake-time. Ends in the queue.
  1. Author the four-stop grass ramp in palette.ts, centred where the grade left it.
  2. Macro mottle in the painter, two octaves, driving ramp position rather than brightness.
  3. Stipple the grass↔sand and grass↔dirt boundaries using the coverage field that already exists.
  4. Revive palette.seasons.*.ground as four ramps; retire SEASON_GRADE.ground. One owner for the question.
  5. Capture the four seasons at one framing and file them together.
3 · Give it lifeNew systems. Costs draw calls.
  1. Wear field on the WetnessField pattern: accumulate on traffic, decay daily, quantise to levels in the shader.
  2. Ground cover — one InstancedMesh, blue-noise placement, density from the macro field, suppressed by wear.
  3. Clover and flowers as accents in the same buffer, at a fraction of the density.
  4. Tie wear to the letter routes, so the path the Postmaster walks every day is the path that shows.

The gate

Three assertions, in the shape island-field.mjs and water-gate.mjs use. Each one fails today, with the number it fails by. A palette grade, a swatch change or a new piece kind can all silently break grass again; this is what would catch it.

AssertionWhat it measuresTodayThresholdVerdict
SEAMGrass border, in world units, where the painted value steps more than 4% and the ground steps less than 0.4 u56.5 u0 ufail
DETAILThe detail sheet's effective swing on the shipped grass swatch, as a fraction of its authored swing — evaluated through the same sRGB→linear path the shader uses0.05≥ 0.80fail
SPREADOver grass texels in the baked sheet, before the shader's neutral breakup: share held by the single most common colour, and distinct colours present57.5% / 44≤ 12% / ≥ 400fail
NYQUIST (advisory)Share of the detail sheet's grass-channel amplitude sitting above 8 cycles per tile at the default camera51%≤ 25%warn
SPREAD is the one that matters. SEAM and DETAIL are specific enough to be gamed by the fix that satisfies them. "No colour on the lawn holds more than an eighth of it" is a property of the result, and no amount of re-plumbing satisfies it without the lawn actually having variation in it. The survey already computes all four numbers — scripts/grass-survey.mjs is the measuring half of this gate.

What this doesn't claim

Nothing here is built.

No engine code changed for this page. The only files it produced are grass/index.html, scripts/grass-survey.mjs, and the frames and JSON in docs/review-queue/grass/.

Nothing here is approved.

Under the jury rule in AGENTS.md, every mockup on this page is a proposal drawn by the thing proposing it. The right-hand studio panel is not evidence that the proposal is good — only that it is specific.

The mockups are 2D.

The studio renders the ground plane at the measured screen scale, which is honest about the base and detail sheets because those are flat textures. It is not honest about layer 5 — tufts are 3D and will catch light the flat marks here don't.

One map.

Every seam number is seed-80085, the map DEFAULT_TEMPLATE_ID ships. The defect is structural, so the other seven maps will have their own totals; the survey walks whichever map is loaded.

The reference section is observation.

The Animal Crossing behaviours listed are what the shipped games visibly do, described from play. No claim is made about how they are implemented.

Grass is canon-independent.

Ground rendering survives either outcome of the POSTMARK/Kindly Forward question, which is why this work is safe to scope now. It touches no cast, map, dialogue or letter.
