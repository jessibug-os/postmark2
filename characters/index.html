Proposed new direction

Kindly Forward

Build a village on St. Mystere and help stranded souls finish their business before the island forgets how to send them onward.

The dramatic engine: everyone here is waiting for a letter. Receiving it may help them live more fully in the village—or make them ready to leave it.
Preproduction, not live canonThe current POSTMARK build still contains the robot cast and original story. The Kindly Forward cast is summoned into prepared homes; this route develops that pivot without pretending implementation has happened.

Visual and emotional direction

Cozy first, but death is not merely wallpaper. The village is a real community where people cook, garden, argue and celebrate while deciding whether they are ready to cross the river.

  • Domestic warmth against cosmic distance. Tea, muddy boots, garden work and badly folded laundry happen beneath an unknowable sky.
  • Human spirits, not generic translucent NPCs. Clothing, posture, age, gesture and possessions carry identity before glow effects do.
  • Leaving is not failure. A resident’s departure can be a completed relationship rather than lost content.
  • Staying is also valid. Nobody is pressured to “move on” for the player’s satisfaction.
  • The postmaster accompanies rather than saves. She delivers truth and offers community; each resident chooses what follows.
  • At gameplay scale, silhouette wins. Period detail belongs in textures and close-ups, never as compensation for same-shaped bodies.

The Postmaster

The one person on the route who may still be alive. Her uncertainty is the long mystery, but her daily purpose is concrete: sort the impossible mail, maintain the village, and stand beside people when an envelope changes everything.

Playable identity

Surface: composed, practical and unusually calm around the dead. She notices procedures before emotions and uses work to postpone questions about herself.

Contradiction: she helps everyone open their letter while refusing to investigate why no letter has ever arrived for her.

Arc: caretaker → indispensable postmaster → frightened possible resident → someone who chooses her relationship to the village without treating mortality as a twist prize.

Daily animation: ferry travel, dock wake, fog stagger, sorting, stamping, bundling, carrying, bell-ringing, handwriting and quiet fatigue. Gathering, fishing and gardening remain, but postal labor becomes equally visible.

Silhouette: oversized head, long tapering hair, flared knee-length coat and prominent crossbody bag. Front and back reads must both work.

Unresolved design question: whether she entered after a near-death event, is already dead without knowing, or belongs to a rare class of living postmasters. Do not answer this during character modelling.

Quest line · The first route

Recruit: name the island, survive the ferry fog, meet Albert and repair the ruined office. Reveal: the summons ledger has no entry for the Postmaster, only a blank space where her destination should be. Pressure: every resident’s letter makes her more necessary to the village and less able to avoid her own absence. Choice: investigate the missing postmaster record, keep serving without an answer, or let the residents help search. Resolution: she chooses whether she is a visitor, a resident or something between those categories; the answer is relational, not a twist reward.

Eight residents

Each resident has an everyday role, a private contradiction and one withheld letter. The player first prepares a home, then summons that soul from St. Mystere’s backlog; the letter reveals truth, and friendship determines how they live with it.

01 · Civic anchor

Albert

Administrative clerk
Forward lean · huge satchel · fluttering tags
Public self

Cheerful, precise and unfailingly useful. He greets arrivals before they realize they have arrived, then apologizes for the state of the office.

Private contradiction

He knows everyone’s destination except his own and suspects his usefulness is the only reason he exists.

Daily life

Sorts the backlog, updates the arrival board, patrols addresses, repairs labels and jumps at every sound from the dock.

Relationship need

Someone who will spend time with him after the work is finished and ask a question he cannot answer from a ledger.

The letter

Addressed in his own handwriting, sealed before his memory begins. He has hidden it in the undeliverable drawer.

Arc / choice

He must decide whether not knowing his destination makes him incomplete—or free to choose a home.

Animation identity

Short quick steps, papers caught midair, compulsive straightening, smile recovery after tiny freezes.

Intro function

Albert explains the backlog. He is more frightened by the Postmaster than she is of him, and that warmth breaks the opening’s eerie spell.

Quest line · The undeliverable clerk

Recruit: repair the arrival board and recover Albert’s missing drawer key. Reveal: his sealed letter is written in his own hand, but he cannot remember sending it. Pressure: Maeve’s archive says Albert is already listed as “pending departure.” Choice: deliver the letter, keep it sealed, or ask Albert to open it himself. Resolution: he stops treating usefulness as proof of belonging and chooses whether to remain the island’s first resident.

02 · First mystery

Mira

The lost child
Raincoat triangle · toy · stubborn reach
Public self

Bright, bossy and convinced everyone is overreacting. She is lost, not dead, and her mother is coming.

Private contradiction

She remembers waiting but not the journey, and refuses to open the suitcase that may contain the answer.

Daily life

Maps shortcuts, adopts insects, reorganizes adult plans, leaves snacks for newcomers and plays near the boarding house.

Relationship need

Adults who tell her the truth without using truth as permission to abandon her.

The letter

A lifetime of short notes written by her mother after Mira vanished, ending only when her mother finally died.

Arc / choice

She learns that being remembered is not the same as being retrievable, then chooses a new form of family.

Animation identity

Fast starts, abrupt stops, toy consultations, coat-sleeve hands and brave poses that collapse when unwatched.

Production warning

Never aestheticize her death. Her humor, agency and ordinary childhood must dominate.

Quest line · The suitcase with no address

Recruit: build a small home with a play corner and map the safe paths Mira keeps claiming to know. Reveal: her mother’s letters prove she was searched for and remembered, but cannot restore the missing journey. Pressure: Mira insists the final letter be delivered to a place that no longer exists. Choice: open the suitcase with her, wait until she asks, or leave it closed. Resolution: she names the village as a family she is allowed to choose, without pretending it replaces her mother.

03 · Threshold keeper

Old Rowan

Ferryman
Wide hat · bowed coat · diagonal pole
Public self

Patient, dryly funny and trusted by everyone. He has guided countless spirits across the river.

Private contradiction

He is terrified to cross himself and disguises fear as responsibility toward the next passenger.

Daily life

Maintains the ferry, reads currents, teaches knots, fishes before dawn and escorts residents to difficult appointments.

Relationship need

Peers who permit him to be frightened instead of treating him as the village’s unshakable elder.

The letter

From the apprentice who took over his living-world ferry and spent a lifetime honoring what Rowan taught him.

Arc / choice

He accepts that the river will continue without him. Whether he crosses immediately remains his decision.

Animation identity

Heavy planted steps, practiced rope work, lantern shielding, understated laughter and a frozen foot at the gangplank.

Production warning

The oar must not be his only identifier; hat, shoulders and bowed center of mass carry the silhouette.

Quest line · The ferryman’s last crossing

Recruit: restore a ferry berth and gather rope, pitch and a lantern for the dead landing. Reveal: Rowan’s apprentice completed the living-world route and wrote to thank him. Pressure: every repaired bridge makes Rowan’s own crossing harder to postpone. Choice: ask him to carry the next soul, accompany him to the gangplank, or let him set the schedule. Resolution: Rowan accepts that guiding others does not require being fearless and chooses his own departure date.

04 · Self-authorship

Liliane

Heiress and reluctant patron
Bell gown · heirloom weight · guarded hands
Public self

Elegant, educated and perfectly mannered. She was trained to be the face of every room her father owned.

Private contradiction

Her whole life was shaped by what men wanted for her, and death has not made it easier to accept that this may be all the life she got.

Daily life

Catalogues heirlooms, organizes the music hall, corrects etiquette, avoids unstructured choices and quietly watches Vittorio play.

Relationship need

Someone who will not turn her into a patron, symbol or project. Vittorio offers music without asking her to perform correctly.

The letter

A final instruction from her father, still expecting her to accept a marriage, donation or public role she never chose.

Arc / choice

She discovers music with Vittorio as a form of expression rather than obedience, then makes one choice no one can call dutiful.

Animation identity

Controlled posture, gloved hands, practiced smiles and increasingly loose, surprised musical gestures.

Production warning

She is not a second musician at the start. Her silhouette is inherited clothing and withheld movement; the music arrives through the arc.

Quest line · A room of her own

Recruit: let Liliane choose and furnish her home plot instead of assigning her an estate. Reveal: her father’s final letter demands one last dutiful marriage, donation or public role. Pressure: every villager has an opinion about what a woman with her resources should build. Choice: help her host a formal recital, hide an improvised rehearsal, or refuse the role of patron entirely. Resolution: Vittorio gives her a medium without an approved result; she makes one song and one life choice that belong to her.

05 · Care

Dr. Amina

Field doctor
Compact square stance · rolled sleeves · medical bag
Public self

Direct, capable and still on call. She checks people who no longer possess pulses and prescribes rest anyway.

Private contradiction

She cannot stop triaging because stillness would force her to remember the people she could not save.

Daily life

Runs the clinic, gathers herbs with Niko, conducts wellness rounds and quietly monitors Mira’s sleep.

Relationship need

Care that is offered without requiring her to first become injured, useful or exhausted.

The letter

From a patient she assumed had died, describing children, grandchildren and an ordinary long life made possible by her work.

Arc / choice

She learns that a healer’s life cannot be measured only by losses and allows the clinic to become a home.

Animation identity

Efficient clipped turns, sleeve-pinning, pulse habit, bag inventory, exhausted shoulder release.

Production warning

Shorten and square her mass. A long elegant coat would collapse her into Liliane and Maeve.

Quest line · The clinic that can close

Recruit: build the clinic and gather herbs, clean cloth and a bell for its first rest ward. Reveal: a former patient writes that Amina’s care gave them decades she never witnessed. Pressure: new residents keep presenting ordinary needs as emergencies. Choice: ask Amina to take a day off, help her train a caretaker, or keep the clinic open around the clock. Resolution: she changes the clinic from emergency station into a place for rest, companionship and care that does not require crisis.

06 · Seasons

Niko

Gardener
Low sunhat · layered apron · garden cart
Public self

Gentle, observant and cheerfully unconcerned with schedules. His greenhouse remembers seasons out of order.

Private contradiction

He cultivates change while secretly arranging the garden to reproduce one final afternoon exactly.

Daily life

Tends seasonal beds, delivers flowers, preserves seeds, maintains graves nobody visits and trades remedies with Amina.

Relationship need

People willing to make new memories in the garden rather than admiring only the preserved old one.

The letter

A packet of seeds and a note from the grandchild who transformed his neglected plot into a community garden.

Arc / choice

He plants something that has never grown before in the village and accepts an unfamiliar spring.

Animation identity

Slow kneeling labor, hat adjustment, soil testing, plant-responsive gestures and quiet delight.

Production warning

Keep foliage secondary. Niko must remain readable when seasonal plants or the cart are absent.

Quest line · The garden that refuses to repeat

Recruit: clear a greenhouse site and help Niko establish beds that are not copies of his remembered afternoon. Reveal: his grandchild turned the old plot into a community garden and sent seeds from it. Pressure: the perfect recreated afternoon keeps crowding out new growth. Choice: preserve the old beds, plant the unknown seeds, or invite villagers to design a shared plot. Resolution: Niko grows something he has never seen and accepts that loving the past does not require living inside it.

07 · Memory

Maeve

Archivist
Tall shawl column · label trails · reading hunch
Public self

Exacting, curious and capable of locating any record except the one she was originally searching for.

Private contradiction

She carries labels for memories she cannot place and fears cataloguing them incorrectly will erase someone twice.

Daily life

Runs the archive, interviews arrivals, repairs letters, organizes exhibitions and argues classification with Albert.

Relationship need

Permission to say “I don’t know” without surrendering her authority or the value of incomplete records.

The letter

A damaged institutional notice proving the archive she devoted her life to deliberately removed her authorship.

Arc / choice

She stops treating official records as neutral truth and begins an oral archive authored by the village itself.

Animation identity

Label tying, paper repair, glasses search, investigative lean and precise indignation.

Production warning

Her stacked vertical silhouette needs dangling labels and a strong hunch, not just another long dress.

Quest line · The archive without an owner

Recruit: restore the archive and collect labels, shelves and testimony from the first residents. Reveal: an institutional notice proves the archive removed Maeve’s authorship while keeping her work. Pressure: Albert wants one official index; the villagers keep producing memories that cannot fit it. Choice: preserve the institution’s categories, rewrite them, or begin an oral archive beside them. Resolution: Maeve accepts that incomplete records can still be honest and gives the village authorship of its own history.

08 · Connection

Vittorio

Wandering musician
Instrument diagonal · split tails · airborne letters
Public self

Flirtatious, generous and impossible to embarrass. He performs letters addressed to strangers as songs.

Private contradiction

He makes intimacy from other people’s words because he never learned to speak plainly in his own.

Daily life

Busks in the square, teaches Mira songs, accompanies departures and turns overheard rhythms into festival music.

Relationship need

Someone who will reject the performance and wait for the unadorned sentence beneath it.

The letter

Addressed to another man with Vittorio’s name: a misdelivery he has kept because its declaration of love feels dangerously personal.

Arc / choice

He delivers the letter to its rightful recipient, then writes the first honest message under his own name.

Animation identity

Large rhythmic stride, instrument-leading turns, coat-tail arcs, finger flourishes and sudden sincere stillness.

Production warning

Break similarity with Albert through broad theatrical movement, coat tails and a dominant instrument diagonal.

Quest line · A song under his own name

Recruit: build the music hall or a small performance corner and recover the instrument he left at the ferry landing. Reveal: his treasured letter was addressed to another man with his name, and he kept it because its honesty felt borrowed but safe. Pressure: Liliane asks him for a song that cannot hide behind someone else’s words. Choice: deliver the letter, perform it, or write a new answer before anyone hears it. Resolution: Vittorio gives the letter to its rightful recipient, then plays one plain message under his own name and helps Liliane make a song that belongs to neither of them at first.

Ensemble story spine

The eight letters remain the fixed content spine. Depth comes from how the same residents change around those deliveries, not from continually adding newcomers.

Act I · Arrival

A village that waits

  • The Postmaster reaches an office that already knows her title.
  • Albert teaches the rules while concealing his own letter.
  • Mira supplies the first obvious moral problem: truth without care is cruelty.
  • The ferry establishes that residents can leave, but nobody is forced.
Act II · Delivery

Truth enters daily life

  • Restored buildings let residents practice identities beyond waiting.
  • Letters arrive through memory landscapes tied to existing activities.
  • Deliveries change schedules, relationships, expressions and environments.
  • The Postmaster notices the absence of any mail addressed to herself.
Act III · Forwarding

A town, not a waiting room

  • Albert’s hidden letter exposes the institution behind the village.
  • Residents make different choices: cross, stay, postpone or return for visits.
  • The village writes its own charter rather than obeying an unseen destination system.
  • The Postmaster chooses a relationship to the town; her metaphysical status does not substitute for that choice.

Supernatural rules

Consistent rules keep the afterlife from becoming convenient dream logic.

Spirits are mostly solid. Faces, hands and silhouettes remain readable. Translucency appears only at edges, during memory loss or near the river.
Letters are materially real. They cannot be forged by wishing. A letter's contents belong to its addressee, and the Postmaster does not open what she carries — a matter of custody she keeps, not a rule the world enforces.
Memory is incomplete, not false on demand. Contradictions indicate perspective, trauma or missing context—not arbitrary twists.
The river is consent. Nobody can push, trick or accidentally lose someone across it. A crossing requires a clear personal choice.
Residents can stay. Readiness is not a progression meter. Community in the village is a legitimate continuing life.
Warmth follows connection. Spirits gain color and environmental influence through relationships, but do not become “more alive” as a moral reward.
Food and sleep are meaningful rituals. Spirits may not biologically require them, but shared meals and domestic routines remain emotionally real.
The Postmaster does not adjudicate worth. Her authority concerns custody and delivery, never deciding who deserves passage or forgiveness.

Cutscene roles — reduced packets

A speaking part is not a cast member. Characters who appear only in an authored sequence get a reduced packet sized to what they actually do on screen. They do not count against the frozen eight, and they do not enter the production order above.

The ferry recruit (/story) is the first and currently only one. He carries the opening’s load-bearing line — “I didn’t know they opened that office back up” — and he is the last ordinary goodbye before the fog, so he must read as a real person. He is also seated on a bench for nearly all of it.

His packet: one silhouette, a seated idle with one fidget, two talk registers (friendly, then unsettled), the route-card double-check, and a single stand-and-walk-off. No locomotion set, no reaction matrix, no daily work loop, no relationship scene.

Production order: after the Postmaster, before Mira — he is required by the opening slice, and the opening slice gates everything else.
