PLAN · CHARACTER GENERATOR

Everyone in town,
from one kit.

The player, the six neighbours, and every ferry passenger should come from the same parts, the same skeleton and the same animations, described by a small recipe.

Today each character is hand-built in its own Blender file and animated one at a time. That made nine polished people. It cannot make a player creator, a wardrobe, or a town that is different in every save.

01 / WHERE WE ARE

Good foundations, built one person at a time.

The repo already agrees on more than it seems. Every humanoid uses the same equipment-v1 skeleton and ships the same clip set, so holding a tool works on everyone. Faces are already painted from a short list of settings in chibi_faces.py, and FaceRig swaps expressions by swapping textures.

The cost is everywhere else. Bodies, hair and clothes are modelled by hand per character, re-saved through a dozen or more versions, then animated separately on each body. The player model in the game is still a code-built placeholder whose only customisation is re-tinting the coat.

  • 10 hand-authored characters, each with its own .blend and 12–20 saved source versions
  • 14 bones in the shared rig, with hand sockets for equipment
  • 14 clips authored again for every body: idle, walk, run, arm layers, holds
  • 1,200–4,400 triangles per character, against the 600–900 budget in CONVENTION.md
  • 35–40 separate meshes per character, so dozens of draw calls each

02 / STYLE TARGET

What the reference asks for.

Proportion

About two and a half heads tall. The head is close to half the height, round rather than boxy. Short legs, a soft pear-shaped torso, stubby arms with mitten hands. Children are the same shape, only smaller.

Face

Small dark eyes set wide and low, tiny nose, warm blush, mouth only when it is expressing something. Painted, not modelled. This is close to what chibi_faces.py already paints.

Silhouette

People are read by their outlines: a flat cap, a wide straw brim, a bun, an apron over a skirt, suspenders, big boots. Hats and hair carry most of the identity, so they need the most variety.

Surface

Soft cloth colour with a few painted folds and seams; trims and buttons are painted, not modelled. Keeps the retro look inside the triangle budget.

Palette

Muted, earthy and warm, with one accent colour per outfit. Colours come from the project palette in src/art/palette.ts, never free RGB, so every combination sits in the world.

Not in scope

Realistic bodies, gender-coded body types, and fine detail that disappears at the gameplay camera distance.

03 / APPROACH

Author parts in Blender. Assemble people in the game.

A recipe (a short JSON description) names a body, a face, hair, clothes and colours. The game assembles the character from a kit of parts at load time. The same recipe drives the player, the named cast, and seeded townsfolk.

04 / THE KIT

Layer by layer.

1 Body frames

Four base bodies on one skeleton: child, adult, sturdy and elder (slight stoop, softer). Each comes with a few blend shapes: height, girth, belly and shoulder width. A frame changes bone lengths; blend shapes change the surface only, so animations survive both.

~350 tris per body, mitten hands, no separate fingers.

2 Head

Three or four head shapes (round, oval, soft-square, wide) plus ears and nose variants. Heads are UV-mapped to the same face rectangle so any face can go on any head.

3 Face

Painted at runtime on a canvas, as FaceRig already does. The picker chooses eyes, brows, nose, mouth, blush, freckles, moles, wrinkles, glasses and facial hair. Expressions still swap textures. Port the chibi_faces.py settings to TypeScript so Blender and the game share one painter.

4 Hair

Separate meshes attached to the head bone. 12–16 styles to start: bun, braids, crop, curls, bob, long, bald, beard sets. Each style also ships a hat-compatible version squashed to fit under a brim, so any hat can go with any hair.

5 Clothing: texture first

Most clothing is painted onto the body: shirts, sweaters, trousers, stockings. Geometry is added only where the outline changes: skirts, aprons, coat tails, rolled cuffs, boots. Every garment shell lists the body regions it covers, so the assembler drops the hidden body faces and nothing pokes through.

Slots: top · bottom · outer · shoes · hat · face accessory · bag.

6 Colour

Parts are painted in greyscale shading with a few colour-region masks. A recipe assigns palette colours to each region, and the assembler recolours on a canvas once. No per-frame shader work is needed, which avoids the shader-cache-key trap in RetroMaterial.

Every kit part is a Blender object with custom properties (slot, frames it fits, regions it hides, colour regions, triangle count), the same way the postmaster's source parts already carry uv_mode and atlas. A validation script refuses parts that are missing them.

05 / THE RECIPE

A person is about thirty lines.

The recipe is the only thing saved for a character. Content packs ship recipes for the named cast. The save file stores the player's recipe and the seed for everyone generated. Changing outfit means editing a recipe and reassembling.

Ids point into the kit, and colours are palette keys. Recipes carry a kit version so an old save can be upgraded when parts are renamed, the same way .island templates carry a library fingerprint.

CastMember.model in src/content/types.ts becomes look: CharacterRecipe, and the per-character clip loaders go away.

06 / ANIMATION

Animate four bodies, not every person.

One skeleton, four frames

Keep the 14-bone equipment-v1 layout and its hand sockets. Author the clip set once per frame, so four times instead of ten, rising to dozens of people. Blend shapes don't move bones, so clips need no per-person changes. author_locomotion.py already derives bounce from leg length, which is exactly how it should scale across frames.

Clips the new game needs

The existing set covers walking, running and holding things. Little Harbor also needs: sit and stand, seated talk, eat and drink, carry a tray, wipe a counter, play cards, shelve, hammer, sweep, wave across the street, and laugh. Add a knee to extra_leg_* while you're there; the locomotion script already notes the foot drags without one.

Personality without bespoke clips

Gait settings (rate, bounce, sway, posture) already exist on CastMember. Keep those, plus one idle quirk per recipe, as the way residents move differently.

Clothes that move

Skirts, aprons and coat tails get one extra bone or a simple spring sway in code. Nothing is cloth-simulated.

07 / IN THE GAME

Assemble once, then it's one mesh.

  1. Load the kit. One kit.glb holds every part and the four frames, or one file per category once it grows.
  2. Pick parts. Resolve the recipe: frame, head, hair (or its hat-compatible version), garment shells. Drop the body faces each garment hides.
  3. Paint one texture. Composite skin, painted clothing, masks and palette colours onto a single 256 px canvas per character. Faces stay a separate small canvas so expression swaps stay cheap.
  4. Merge. Combine the chosen skinned pieces into one mesh on one skeleton: one or two draw calls per person instead of 35–40.
  5. Cache. Key the result by recipe hash, so twenty ferry passengers sharing a look cost one build.
  6. Hand off. The output looks exactly like today's .glb to SkeletalAnimator, EquipmentController and FaceRig, so the NPC and player code doesn't need to know where it came from.

08 / GENERATING THE TOWN

Seeded, but never random-looking.

Each save already has an island seed. The same seed should produce the people who aren't named cast: ferry commuters, market visitors, the family in the house on the hill.

Wardrobes, not dice

Generation starts from a role and an age (fisher, student, retiree, visitor) and draws from that role's wardrobe. An outfit is a curated set of parts with colour rules, so nobody comes out in a clown suit.

Colour harmony

Each outfit takes one base colour family and one accent from palette groups that are known to work together. Skin, hair and eyes come from separate natural ranges.

Distinct from the cast

Generated people must differ from every named resident in at least their hat-or-hair outline and main colour, so nobody confuses a stranger with Basil from across the street.

Named cast stay fixed

Ada, Basil, June, Omar, Nell and Pip are hand-written recipes in the content pack, identical in every save. Only their homes and routines move with the island.

Changes over time

Recipes can carry seasonal outfit overrides: scarves in winter, rolled sleeves in summer, rain gear on wet days. The weather system already knows when it's raining.

Reproducible

Same seed, same people. Never store generated recipes in the save; store the seed plus anything the player changed, such as a gift someone now wears.

09 / THE PLAYER CREATOR

A photo for the ferry pass, then a wardrobe.

Character creation happens inside the fiction: before boarding, you fill in your ferry pass and pick the photo. Later, a mirror and wardrobe in your room let you change anything. Hair can be changed at a barber's chair, if the town gets one.

There's no gender selection and no part locked to a body type. Every frame, face, hair and garment is open to everyone, and pronouns are a separate question on the pass.

Clothes become part of the economy. The creator starts you with a small closet. More arrives through the secondhand shop, gifts from neighbours, and things you make at the repair yard. This gives the money loop from the direction page something personal to buy.

10 / BUILD ORDER

Each step ends with something you can look at.

  1. Lock the proportions

    Block out the four frames in Blender as grey shapes beside the style target and the current cast. Agree on head size, leg length and hand size before any detail.

    Done when: a turnaround sheet of the four frames is approved.

  2. Skeleton and clips

    Rig the frames on equipment-v1 (plus knees), move the existing clip set over, check holds still land in the hands.

    Done when: four grey bodies walk, run, sit and hold a pot in the game.

  3. Face painter in TypeScript

    One painter for Blender previews and the runtime, built from FaceRig and chibi_faces.py. Twenty face parts to start.

    Done when: the existing cast's faces can be reproduced from recipes.

  4. Assembler and recipe schema

    Load the kit, resolve parts, paint one texture, merge one mesh, cache by hash. Replace this plan page with the live lab: recipe editor, randomise, turnaround, clip player.

    Done when: /chargen builds a character from JSON at the gameplay camera size.

  5. First wardrobe

    Painted tops and bottoms, the first garment shells (apron, skirt, overalls, coat), six hats, twelve hair styles with hat-compatible versions.

    Done when: the six named residents are built as recipes and recognisable at gameplay distance.

  6. Into the game

    Switch CastMember to recipes, give the player a recipe in the save, retire PlayerModel.ts.

    Done when: the save, golden and island tests pass with recipe-built characters.

  7. Seeded townsfolk

    Roles, wardrobes, colour rules, cast-distinctness check, seasonal overrides.

    Done when: two different seeds produce different but equally believable crowds of 20.

  8. Creator and wardrobe in the game

    Ferry-pass creator, mirror and wardrobe, clothing as shop stock and gifts.

    Done when: a new player can make themselves and change outfit without leaving the game.

11 / RISKS AND OPEN QUESTIONS

Where this could go wrong.

Kit people look generic

The hand-built cast has charm because each one was fussed over. The answer is enough hair and hat variety, faces with character, and a few one-off parts reserved for the named residents. Test early by rebuilding Mira from the kit and comparing the two side by side.

Clipping between layers

The usual failure of modular characters. Painting most clothing on, hiding covered body faces, and having hat-compatible hair versions removes most of it. Add a test that renders every hat × hair pair and every shell on every frame.

Four frames may be too few

The style target's people are almost all one shape, which supports starting small. Add a frame only when a recipe truly can't be reached with blend shapes.

What happens to the current cast?

Albert, Mira and the others belong to the retired story. Keep their files as reference for quality and painting style, but don't migrate them. The new cast starts as recipes.

Open: how much does the player customise?

Body shape sliders or just frame choice? Free colour or palette only? This plan assumes frames plus three sliders and palette-only colour.

Open: animals

The style target has a dog and a cat. Animals need a separate small kit on their own skeleton; that's out of scope here, but worth planning next.

A stranger on the ferry, your neighbour, and the face in your mirror should all look like they were made by the same hands.
